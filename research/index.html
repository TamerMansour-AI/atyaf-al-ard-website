Continuity of Life and Culture in Palestine – From Ancient Jericho to the Present

Theme: deep history, Canaanite civilization, Sea Peoples, population continuity, women’s agency.

This research brief brings together recent findings from ancient DNA, archaeology, and anthropology to rethink the history of Palestine. Instead of a story of repeated population replacement, it shows a strong continuity of local communities from the Neolithic city of Jericho, through the Canaanite and early Philistine periods, and into the lives of today’s Palestinians.

  • Ancient Jericho (Tell es-Sultan) as one of the earliest continuously inhabited towns in the world, with early farming, stone walls, a monumental tower, and complex ancestor rituals.
  • Bronze Age Canaan as a hybrid cultural hub, blending Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Anatolian and Aegean influences into its own arts, music, and religious practices.
  • Sea Peoples / early Philistines arriving around 1200 BCE as a limited migration that quickly mixed with local Canaanite populations, creating a hybrid culture rather than replacing the inhabitants.
  • Genetic continuity showing that modern Palestinians and other Levantine peoples derive the vast majority of their ancestry from Bronze and Iron Age populations of the region.
  • Women’s roles in ancient Canaan as queens, landowners, priestesses, and diplomatic actors, challenging the idea of a purely patriarchal past.
