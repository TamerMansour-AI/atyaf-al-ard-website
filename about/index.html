About Atyaf Al Ard

Atyaf Al Ard is a long-term Palestinian research and storytelling lab. We explore the deep history of this land — from ancient populations to today’s people — and translate that knowledge into films, images, music, and learning spaces powered by AI.

Why this project exists

For more than a century, the story of Palestine has been reduced to headlines, conflicts, and borrowed narratives. Atyaf Al Ard was created to do the opposite:

  • to start from the land itself: its layers, climate, and archaeology,
  • to listen to science: genetics, history, and anthropology,
  • and to reconnect all of this with living Palestinians today.

Our goal is not nostalgia and not propaganda. Our goal is to build a careful, well-sourced narrative that shows Palestinians as the continuous people of this land, across thousands of years.

The founders

Mohammed Abu Mualiq is a writer, researcher, and educational specialist, as well as a visual storyteller working with AI-assisted imagery. He focuses on human stories, memory, and daily life in Palestine, and on turning complex ideas into accessible narratives and learning experiences.

Tamer Mansour is an AI media creator, educator, and consultant. He builds workflows that combine research, scripting, image and video generation, and interactive tools, with a focus on Palestinian history and education.

Together, they founded Atyaf Al Ard as a space where:

  • research is done with scientific rigor,
  • media is created with cinematic ambition,
  • and AI is treated as a creative partner, not a replacement for people.

How we work

The project moves along three connected streams:

  1. Research lab
    Genetic continuity studies, archaeological and historical research, and social and cultural histories of daily life in Palestine. Each research thread is documented and organised in tools like NotebookLM, so that others can explore the sources, not only the conclusions.
  2. Media & experiences
    Short films and scripted scenes, visual timelines and illustration series, and music and soundscapes inspired by each era. AI helps with drafting, visualising, and experimenting — but every piece is checked against real sources and expert work.
  3. Youth & community
    Training a small youth team to help with research, run the project’s social media and documentation, and open parts of the process to schools, workshops, and local creators.

Where we are now

Phase 1 of Atyaf Al Ard is still in progress. Right now we are:

  • finishing our first major research threads (such as genetic continuity studies),
  • shaping the core visual language of the project,
  • building the website and research spaces for public use,
  • and designing the first training path for the youth team.

The project is flexible by design. We can grow toward a full documentary series, a set of short films, educational kits for schools, or a research-based archive — depending on the partners and support that join us.
